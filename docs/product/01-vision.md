# 01 — Vision

## One line

Cat-ch turns everyday walks into a gentle habit of discovering real cats,
adopting them as unique virtual companions, and — through the community it
builds — measurably improving the lives of real cats around the world.

**Tagline:** *Every Cat Has a Story. Every Player Can Make a Difference.*

## The feeling we are selling

Not a race, not a fight, not a slot machine. Cat-ch should feel like the pleasant
surprise of turning a corner and meeting a cat sunning itself on a wall — plus
the quiet satisfaction of a well-tended collection and the knowledge that your
play does real good. Cozy, wholesome, relaxing, rewarding, community-driven.

## Design pillars

Every feature decision should be traceable to one of these. If a feature serves
none of them, it does not belong in Cat-ch.

1. **Discovery** — the world is full of cats, and finding them should feel
   delightful. Exploration is the engine; the map and camera are the heart.
2. **Compassion** — kindness to real animals is rewarded; cruelty, harassment,
   and disturbance are never mechanics. Real-world welfare is the point, not a
   marketing veneer.
3. **Coziness** — low-pressure pacing. Neglect shifts a companion's *mood*, it
   never inflicts harsh punishment or loss. Players can put the game down guilt-free.
4. **Uniqueness** — every captured cat is visually and behaviorally
   one-of-a-kind, a memento of a specific real animal met at a specific place and
   time. The CatDex becomes a journal of real memories.
5. **Transparency** — where the money goes, which shelters we partner with, and
   how many cats we've helped are all published openly. Trust is a feature.

## Who it's for

- **Primary:** casual, cozy-game and collector audiences (think *Neko Atsume*,
  *Animal Crossing*, *Pokémon GO* players who prefer the collecting to the
  battling). Broad age range; cat lovers of all kinds.
- **Secondary:** animal-welfare-minded people who want a low-effort way to
  contribute, and lapsed location-game players seeking something kinder.

Because the audience will include **minors**, and the app uses **camera and
location**, child-safety and privacy obligations are first-class design
constraints, not afterthoughts — see
[Ethics, Privacy & Safety](../08-ethics-privacy-safety.md).

## Tone & art direction (north star, to be refined with design)

- Warm, rounded, hand-illustrated cozy aesthetic; soft palettes.
- Companions are **stylized and cute**, not photoreal — but recognizably derived
  from the real cat's colors, patterns, and features.
- Copy is gentle and encouraging; never nagging, never fear-based, never
  exploitative of the desire to "not let your pet down."

## Explicit non-goals

Naming these protects the pillars over time:

- **Not competitive or aggressive.** No violent combat; competition (if any) is
  playful and optional.
- **Never pay-to-win.** Monetization is cosmetic and optional. Skill, preparation,
  and personality matter more than spending. See
  [Monetization & Impact](../09-monetization-and-impact.md).
- **Never encourages disturbing animals.** No mechanic rewards chasing, luring,
  cornering, touching, or trespassing. Observation over interaction, always.
- **Not a surveillance product.** We do not build a public registry of where
  specific (often owned or vulnerable) cats live. Location handling is
  privacy-first. See [Ethics, Privacy & Safety](../08-ethics-privacy-safety.md).
- **Not a dark-pattern retention machine.** No guilt loops, no punishing streak
  mechanics, no manipulative FOMO.

## The long-term dream

Cat-ch aspires to be more than a game: a worldwide, wholesome movement that
celebrates cats while creating measurable social impact. Success looks like
players who, years later, remember the real cats they met on neighborhood walks,
travels, and vacations — and an honest annual report showing millions of cats
discovered, shelter adoptions driven through partners, meals funded, treatments
sponsored, and money donated to accredited organizations. Those numbers are
aspirations to *earn*, not promises to print; see
[Monetization & Impact](../09-monetization-and-impact.md) and
[Risks & Open Questions](../10-risks-and-open-questions.md) for the work required
to make them real.
